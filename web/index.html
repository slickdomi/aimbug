Knobs

Strafe: one female drifts sideways and up and down. Static: she sits still. Two females: both are visible at once and pull the fly's steering in two directions. A female that isn't shot flies off after 6 s.

Constant input to the male's P1 courtship-arousal neurons (the pC1_* types). A female in view already makes pIP10 sing a little; arousal makes the song strong enough to pull the trigger. Around 8 mV and up he sings even at an empty room.

Turning speed per Hz of difference between the right and left DNa02 steering neurons. Low values make the fly sluggish; high values make it overshoot and wobble. 0 means no turning, a useful control: aim disappears.

Speed of looking up or down. It follows DNp53, which fires for targets above, balanced against LC4 and LPLC2, which fire for any visible target. A weak spring pulls the view back to the horizon. 0 keeps the view level.

How strong the pIP10 courtship song (left + right, smoothed) must get before the fly fires. It fires once per song bout. Lower values mean more shots. The song rate doesn't depend on where the crosshair is, so this changes how often the fly shoots, not how well.

Recurrent gain of the graded (non-spiking) optic lobe. Higher values carry visual signals further. Above about 2.39 the lamina feedback loops become unstable and activity slowly saturates.

How strongly optic lobe activity drives the spiking visual projection neurons (LC10, LC4, LPLC2, …) that feed the central brain. Too low and the fly is blind; too high and both hemispheres fire regardless of where the target is.

Half the female's body length as seen by the fly, which is how far away she is. The model eye samples the world every 4.8°, so below about 13° she covers too few eye columns and the fly barely sees her.
